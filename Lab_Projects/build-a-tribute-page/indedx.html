Marie Curie – The Pioneer of Radioactivity

Marie Curie was a physicist and chemist who conducted pioneering research on radioactivity. Born in Poland in 1867, she moved to France where she became the first woman to teach at the Sorbonne. She discovered two elements, polonium and radium, and developed the theory of radioactivity—a term she coined herself.

Her dedication to science led to groundbreaking advances in medicine and physics. She was the first woman to win the Nobel Prize and remains the only person to ever win it in two different sciences—Physics and Chemistry.
